Guitar Hero Live, my live rhythm-game show

Bringing Guitar Hero IRL.

My part
My idea. I directed the build.
Read the story
Craft
A mod of the rhythm game Clone Hero (C#/Unity) that follows my setlist, a small Python server the phones and screens talk to (REST/JSON API), a phone sign-up that queues players, a live Top 5 board and the booking video.
How
One challenge: on debut night the big screen ran a few seconds behind the guitar, because every change made the browser draw a fresh page. Now one Chrome window stays open and the server pushes each new screen straight into it over the Chrome DevTools Protocol.

Guitar Hero Live is a homage to how I got started. I wanted my sets to be more of an experience, so I used no-code tools to build my own game that brings people from the audience up to join the band. It had to work from a lot of angles at once: my band and I had to stay in sync, sign-up had to be easy, and the show had to run while I'm performing. Now we're working with multiple booking agencies, and the EPK I made uses DaVinci (through MCP), ffmpeg and Remotion to polish the footage we record at shows.

Result
Debuted Oct 10, 2026. It was a successful event, and we're now booking up 2027, playing around the region.

Claude Code and Codex wrote the code. The booking video was also edited with my agentic MCP editing suite.

UX/UI design, C#/Unity, Python, REST/JSON API, DMX lighting (OSC), Chrome DevTools Protocol

Supercaster

Building the ultimate guitar player's guitar. (It went viral.)

My part
I designed the guitar, and used AI to optimize the acoustics and make a CNC-ready file.
Read the story
Why
I've always played one guitar, and I wanted it to do every sound, in the studio or in a three-hour set. A guitar full of innovations from years of playing and experimenting with it.
Idea
Craft the instrument's ergonomics, electronics and acoustics to be as good as they can possibly be.
Craft
I ran experiments in code on the body's acoustic resonance, tweaking the air chamber, the top geometry and the hole physics. Then I made a passive wiring system that can recreate any electric guitar sound, and even acoustic ones. Lastly, I made the neck play better than any guitar neck in the world.

I designed the Supercaster to be the ultimate version of everything that gets my heart pumping about guitar. I obsessed over every detail and came up with my own pickup and electronics to make the ultimate studio guitar: one that can sound like any other guitar. After it went viral, I started modeling a CNC-ready version in code, pushing the acoustic physics of what an electric guitar can do.

Result
A CNC design that's easy to build and keeps every enhancement, and a kickass guitar that turns heads on every stage I play. Twelve Shorts about it have 1.33M views on YouTube.

Parametric CAD, Python, three.js, FE/BEM simulation

Triple Entendre

One night on a green screen. Can my Claude Code subscription help me edit this video without smelling like AI?

My part
My song and my concept. I directed it, starred in it and ran the edit build.
Read the story
Idea
DaVinci Resolve just added MCP features, but is it ready to help bring the edit to the finish line?
Craft
A three-minute music video made with ffmpeg, Python and Remotion, as well as DaVinci post-processing. No generative AI in the picture. Every shot is keyed off the green screen and turned into flat-color silhouettes. The drawings move at ten frames a second, so they jitter like hand animation, and the color changes land on the drummer's kick.
How
This one took many, many revisions and tweaks by hand. I learned that for creative, AI likely won't close the gap, but as an assistant editor it was amazing. With extensive pre-production, it's a game changer.

Triple Entendre is a song about mixed signals, arranged like 2010s dance-pop, so I made a music video inspired by early iTunes ads. I came up with the concept and directed it. It was also my first real dive into agentic editing, and I pushed the limits of what agents and my Mac can handle. I found a stack of tools that each do what they're best at, and built my strategy from pre-production to the last render.

Result
My final cut, built in code. I gave references and trained an agentic team of assistant editors.

Shot by Craig Martin at In Your Ear Studios.

Green-screen keying, Python, ffmpeg, Remotion, DaVinci Resolve, Claude Code
